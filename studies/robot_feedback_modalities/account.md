# Robot, gripper and load feedback screen

Both predefined fixed calibrations passed at their four withheld wrist orientations, with open and empty-closed gripper conditions. Force residual p95 was 0.000475 N and torque residual p95 0.000380 Nm in each starting state. All quiet windows met the motion criteria and had no unexpected gripper contact. These are deterministic simulation results, not hardware noise or accuracy claims. Both 1,680-step action sequences and every public robot sensor reading replayed exactly.

The six original task traces and two fixed verification sequences then received the same frozen 20 Hz monitor, without changing their actions. Every expected frame was retained: 14,078 monitored motion frames and 10,878 private task-contact geometry rows. The monitor uses only robot pose, requested targets, finger opening/velocity and force/torque; private geometry is added afterward for context. It does not use CoTracker, object identities, rewards or task checks to compute a flag.

| Signal | Original task waypoints flagged | Fixed free-motion waypoints flagged | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Changed compensated wrist load | 52 / 177 | 0 / 54 | Changed load, not necessarily harmful contact |
| Unfinished stagnant motion | 20 / 177 | 0 / 54 | Target remains unfulfilled, not a cause diagnosis |
| Empty-sized stable closure | 7 / 177 | 26 / 54 | Empty or too thin to distinguish; needs explicit policy arming |

The 26 fixed empty-closure flags are expected during intentional empty closure. They show why this must not be an unconditional stop rule. All seven first task flags lacked two-pad frame/tool/stand contact; three still had some gripper contact. Thus “not empty-sized” does not mean “acquired,” and incidental contact does not make a grasp secure. In the second-start timed baseline, the first grasp closes to the empty aperture at step 120, immediately before the 45-step lift starts. That gives a concrete opportunity to interrupt an unproductive phase without choosing a new grasp for Astra.

The load monitor has narrower coverage than whole-arm collision detection. Nine of 52 first load flags had no robot/environment contact geometry at that instant; dynamics, changing held-object load and frame-dependent tare remain confounds. Conversely, during the first-start phase run's wrist recovery (command 16), assembly first becomes transiently false at step 1218 and is lost finally at 1296, with proximal arm/frame contact. Neither load nor stall flags during that command. The wrist sensor is downstream of those arm links. A calibrated wrist load is therefore useful additional information, not an arm-clearance or seating certificate. The prior permanent-loss account remains valid; this screen also preserves earlier transient failures.
