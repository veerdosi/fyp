# Causal point accuracy result

The official scaled-online CoTracker3 configuration retained 97.7% of reference-visible points, but failed the frozen precision and loss gates: accepted current-point error p95 was 5.55 mm against 3 mm, and 24.8% of reference-occluded points were accepted against 5%. Accepted visible median error was 0.327 mm. The result supports better retention than the local-flow prototype, not a reliable precision-control or loss certificate. The periodic and per-frame audits have different measurement schedules, so their percentages are not a matched estimator-effect comparison.

All 212 original queries and 35 motion streams were retained across six episodes and two observed starts. There were 2,757 query endpoints: 1,909 reference-visible, 339 occluded and 509 unknown. Initial references were known for 171 queries and unknown for 41. Unknown references remain in the records and availability ledger; they do not supply accuracy evidence or imply a semantic selection error. Geometry/body correspondence does not establish whether Astra selected the correct task point.

Every original action replayed exactly (10,878 steps). All 49 query camera snapshots matched original RGB, depth and calibration exactly. Source/input hashes and causal endpoint/frame completeness passed. No future frames improved earlier scored outputs, and no stale point was presented as a current measurement. The 22,739-frame query availability ledger contains fresh estimates on 12.1% of motion/query frames. Bootstrap acquisition takes 0.75 simulated seconds; these are periodic measurements, not continuous 20 Hz feedback.

The 644 local tracker forward calls took 703.1 seconds in total. Loading took 0.585 seconds; synchronized steady single-camera call p95 was 1.199 seconds. Total audit wall time was 1,018.2 seconds, including replay/rendering and bookkeeping. These single-camera timings across many streams are distinct from the earlier combined two-camera runtime probe. New Astra calls: zero. The earlier 0.4-second runtime gate remains failed, but it is not a necessary FYP requirement; slow inference could still be useful with paused physics if its measurements were adequate.

## Where the error comes from

Both cameras and both motion classes fail the precision/loss gate. Fixed-camera accepted-error p95 was 5.44 mm, wrist-camera 5.85 mm; translation 3.55 mm and rotation 5.96 mm. Both observed starts fail. Diagnostic body groups cannot be used as a private selector for the executor. In particular, precise tool-hole or stand subsets do not certify the difficult frame surface or the whole task.

Private overlays, reconstructed through original chronological actions, expose a depth-association failure. At `s01_direct`, query 24 / point 5, step 1885, the tracked fixed-camera pixel is only about 0.36 pixels from the reference projection, but its sampled depth is 2.927 m rather than the reference's 0.557 m. The resulting accepted world-point error is 2.401 m. At the wrist-camera frame point, 2.84 pixels of offset samples the table instead of the thin frame, producing 242.5 mm error. An accepted occluded point in `s02_direct` also projects close to the reference but samples background, producing 2.171 m error. Each error and visibility label reproduced exactly. The overlays contain private references for diagnosis only.

Occlusion is not merely an overly strict proxy here. Of 84 accepted occluded endpoints, 57 had actual current-point errors above 3 mm; their error p95 was 24.21 mm. Across all known accepted endpoints, error p95 was 7.42 mm. Tracker visibility alone does not validate the depth surface. Better 2D correspondence therefore does not automatically produce reliable metric feedback at thin silhouettes.

The gates, complete cohort and outputs are preserved. No executor has been built around this unvalidated measurement. The next necessary test broadens feedback beyond point tracking: [robot/contact feedback calibration](../robot_feedback_modalities/decision.md), followed by a recorded sensor-only monitor screen if compensation is supported. Finger opening, robot progress and wrist load have different observable limits; none should be called a grasp or seating certificate without evidence. H1 remains untested in a robot-policy comparison, the wider H3 effect remains unresolved, and H2/H4 remain open. A subsequent matched Astra test uses two new starting states after the working primitive and comparison are frozen.
